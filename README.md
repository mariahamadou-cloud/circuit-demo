12V DC to 240V AC Push-Pull Inverter

A simple, low-cost DC-to-AC power inverter designed in KiCad. This project converts a 12V DC input (from a battery or power supply) into a 240V AC output using an IR2153 self-oscillating driver, IRLZ24 power MOSFETs, and a center-tapped step-up transformer.


Technical Specifications
Input Voltage: 12 V DC
Output Voltage: ≈ 240 V AC
Oscillator/Driver: IR2153 self-oscillating gate driver
Switching Elements: 2 × IRLZ24 N-channel MOSFETs
Transformer: 240 V to 12-0-12 V center-tapped transformer, operated in reverse as a step-up transformer
Input Protection: D3 — 1N4007 reverse-polarity protection diode
Power Indicator: D2 — Power-on LED
Output Indicator: D1 — AC output status LED
Design Tool: KiCad V10.0

Circuit Description :

1. Power Supply and Protection

* The 12 V DC input is supplied through BT1. Diode D3 (1N4007) provides basic reverse-polarity protection for the inverter circuit.

* Capacitors C3 and C4 are used for supply filtering and help reduce voltage fluctuations and switching noise generated by the power stage.

2. Oscillator and Gate Drive

U1 (IR2153) provides the switching oscillator and gate-drive signals required to control the MOSFETs.

* Note : The operating frequency is determined primarily by the timing components connected to the RT and CT pins : RV1, R5, and C1 provide frequency adjustment.

The two gate-drive outputs operate alternately so that Q1 and Q2 switch in opposite phases. Gate resistors are used to limit instantaneous gate-drive current and reduce switching transients.

3. MOSFET Power Stage

Q1 and Q2 alternately switch the two ends of the 12-0-12 V winding toward ground.

4. Transformer and AC Output

The low-voltage 12-0-12 V winding is driven by the MOSFET power stage, while the 240 V winding provides the high-voltage AC output.

* Note : The actual output voltage depends on the transformer ratio, battery voltage, MOSFET voltage drop, switching waveform, transformer losses, and connected load.
